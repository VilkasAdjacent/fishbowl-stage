Day 6: The Girl Who Stopped at the Top of the Steps

Thursday 5 July 2046, night

RIVER LINE FLOOD WARNING. Avoid underpasses, towpaths and canal steps until 06:00. Do not attempt to cross standing water.an emergency alert text, sent to all phones in the old core

Over the old core the rain let up for twenty minutes. The canal yard lay ankle-deep and went flat as glass. Under the old canal bridge Isabel sat on the stone lip with her knees drawn up. The storm had carried her ash down from the lane, gutter by gutter, and left it floating on the yard in grey rafts.

The mascots came out from under the arches by the dozen. They dropped onto the rafts and fought over them, wings and beaks and small round bodies, close enough over her head that she could hear them eat.

None of them looked at her. They had come for what she had put in the gutters, and they were eating it, and that was the whole of it. The flat had watched her all week. The yard did not. She could stay.

Then the wind came back off the river and the rafts broke apart. The mascots went back under the arches. Isabel crawled into the iron hatch in the bridge footing, where the sluice gear had once been, and stayed. She had no coat. Her phone was still on the bathroom tiles in the flat.

At Linden Rise, Amara Nwosu locked her bedroom door and sat on the floor in the dark. She called up the staff, let it go, called it again. She kept it the length of her forearm, the copper at its head turned down to almost nothing. Across the landing the rest of the flat slept through it.

Out on the fringe estates it was only drizzle. At the Tangle the frames held their wire and the polytunnel skirts held their breeze blocks. Hyejin Park shut her shed door and lay down and slept.

At four in the morning Natalie let herself out of Isabel's flat. She had wound fresh kitchen towel around each palm and taped it at the wrist. The rain was sideways again. She went through the old core alone, toward the old canal bridge.

By the top of the canal steps the towel was soaked through. The burns had opened along their old lines, and the cloth had gone pink. She put her right hand to the rail and the fingers would not close. She tried the left. Below her the steps went down into the yard, and the water there was moving again under the wind. The bridge was at the far end of it.

It had been a short walk. It was her hands that settled it, the way her body had settled things for her before, and they had settled it against her.

She stood at the top of the steps with her hands held away from her coat. Across the river the south ridge towers kept up their red pulse.
