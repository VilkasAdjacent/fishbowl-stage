Day 5: The Girl Who Drank the Tea to the Bottom

Thursday 5 July 2046, night

Youth curfew in force from 23:00. Last southbound service departs 22:52.a tram stop display, Old Core line

Rain came sideways off the river and stood in the grates. The flowers under the fire stair had not closed.

Mrs Vella from the ground floor came out with a thermos and two chipped mugs. She poured for Isabel and for Amara Nwosu, who had come down the lane an hour before. She said it was the best thing to happen on the lane in the forty years she had lived on it. She said it twice, and named the neighbours who had gone.

Isabel held her mug and drank it to the bottom. Mrs Vella poured again. The rain went through Isabel's shirt at the shoulders. By the second mug her teeth were going and the tea slopped over the rim.

Amara looked at her phone, then at the time on it. She thanked Mrs Vella, gave back the mug and ran for the tram. The last southbound left at 22:52.

On the fringe estates Hyejin Park weighted the polytunnel skirts with breeze blocks and wired the garden frames shut. One frame lid went over the fence. She let it go.

Isabel set her mug on the step and walked out of the lane. She had no coat and no phone and told no one where she was going. Three streets on she put on the line's shape against a wall and began a cut. Her hands gave out partway. She stayed in the shape a while after, the copper fading at her fingers.

Upstairs, Natalie found the flat empty. The spare coat was on its hook. The phone was on the desk.

She wrapped her weeping palms in kitchen towel, bound it with black electrical tape, and went to the window. The south ridge kept its red pulse through the rain.
