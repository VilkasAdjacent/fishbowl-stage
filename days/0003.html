Day 3: The Girl Who Would Not Stay Put

4 July 2046
Linden Rise Drain Lake. No swimming. No fishing. Dogs on leads at all times.a council sign at the lake's east gate

The desk lamp was the only light in the flat, amber on the wood. Rain drove against the window from the river side. Across the water the south ridge towers kept their red pulse.

Natalie sat with her hands palm-up on a folded towel. The dressings were two days old. Isabel had changed them once and was due to again at one.

Isabel put the roll of gauze down. She asked whether Natalie had taken the cut out of her hands because of last night. She said the word kiss. Then she picked the gauze back up.

Natalie did not answer.

Isabel's phone lit blue on the desk. A post in Bay Sightings, from a handle with no history. Something big near the drain lake at Linden Rise. Is it ok to go alone.

The same minute, in the north lanes, the Hundred and Ninth: Creeping Knotweed grew nerves again, through the crooked seam where it had been closed.

Isabel read the post aloud. She read out the tram line, the stop, the walk from the stop to the east gate, fourteen minutes in this weather.

Natalie stood. She took Isabel's coat off the chair and the phone off the desk, one in each hand. The grip split the burn across both palms. Blood came through the gauze and onto the collar of the coat. She said stay here. She said it twice.

Isabel took her spare coat from the hook by the door. The route was already in her head. She was down the first flight of stairs before Natalie had the door shut behind her.
