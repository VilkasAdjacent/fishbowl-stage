Day 11: The Girl Who Kept Her Head Down Through Every Bell

Sunday 8 July 2046, morning

Next stop, the eastern wards. Please take everything you brought with you.the recorded announcement on the river tram

The next morning the thaw came on under a clear sky, and meltwater ran in the gutters. The air was cold enough to hurt.

The moth mascot had followed Amara Nwosu to Linden Rise. At lunch in the quad it came down onto her cuff, and three more dropped out of the bare plane trees after it and fought over the ash on her sleeve. Nobody else at the table looked up. She pulled her arm under the table, and the moths went with it.

Back in class she put her head down on the desk and pulled her blazer up round her ears. The teacher said her name twice. The girl beside her asked if she was all right. Amara did not lift her head for the roll, or the next bell, or the one after.

The desk was the only place left. They had reached her in the quad, in daylight, with no grate to put the sleeve down. If she answered nothing and moved nothing, there was nothing to pull out of her. Give them nothing at all. That was the test.

She was still down at the last bell.

Elspeth Hale left the north lanes blight off the register. She wrote no line about it, and walked home through the wet to the eastern wards.

Hyejin Park took the river tram out of the city. The canal bridge went by behind her, and the cameras with it.
