Day 17: The Girl Who Would Not Read the Name Aloud

Saturday 14 July 2046, morning

Frost on the points. Eastern wards services suspended until further notice.the River Tram service board

On the Saturday, Isabel climbed shaking out of the hatch under the old canal bridge and walked to her half-done cut in the north lanes, to finish it alone and tell no one. Her hands were already numb with cold.

Light ran along her forearms, and she held the line's shape a beat before setting her staff to the seam. Then her hands went before she did. The staff slid in her fingers; the shape slipped off the blade and would not come back. It was not the cold, she thought.

Elspeth Hale traced the green on the ash under the fire stair to Isabel's flat and knocked. Natalie answered. Elspeth knew her from the bank in 2025. One register line stood blank, The Hundred and Fourth: Serrated Tussock, and Natalie was the voice it waited for.

Elspeth blocked the doorway and asked her to read the name aloud. Natalie kept it behind her teeth and shut the door. In the frost, Elspeth's fingers had gone past feeling the pen. "I beg your pardon," she told the shut door, and went down the stair.

Natalie forced the bathroom latch and went through Iz's phone for where she had gone. There was nothing on it.

At the eastern wards tram stop, Hyejin Park said aloud what she would ask Natalie, the last person she wanted to ask: how to stand in front of 2035. No tram came through the frost, and the day went before she reached Natalie's stair.
