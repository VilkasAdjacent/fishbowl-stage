Day 9: The Girl Who Asked for the Route Home

Friday 6 July 2046, dawn

Frost again tonight on the fringe. Knock on an older neighbour's door. A knock costs nothing.the overnight presenter, River Line call-in radio

Frost had come down on the fringe estates in the night and stayed. Over the old core the sky was hard and pale. The canal yard held its floodwater without a ripple, and ice had closed over it along the walls.

Hyejin Park came down to the old canal bridge at first light. Isabel was out of the hatch, standing at the edge of the yard in the clothes she had worn for two days.

"Can you get me up the steps," Isabel said. "It's twenty minutes to the north lanes. There's a coat at the flat. I just need someone to walk it with me."

Hyejin looked at her. Then she turned and went back up the steps without a word, toward the Tangle.

It was the same silence as the locked bathroom. She had asked it plainly, with the route and the time, and got the back of a coat. So it had been settled up on the lane, and nobody had told her.

Isabel got down into the iron hatch in the bridge footing and pulled the lid shut over her head. It was dark inside and smelled of old grease. She had no coat and no phone. She sat with her knees up and waited out the cold.

At the top of the steps Hyejin heard the iron close, and walked on. At the Tangle she lifted the frost cloth on each frame, one after another, and laid it back.

At Linden Rise, before first bell, Amara Nwosu took wet paper towel into the end stall and shot the bolt. She washed the cut in her forearm until the towel came away clean, wound her tie over it twice and pulled the knot tight with her teeth. She drew her sleeve down to the wrist and came out with nothing on her face.

At breakfast a Continuity canvasser in a soft blue jacket worked the north lanes stair. At Isabel's door he knocked, got nothing, and slid a leaflet under it: Your time, kept kindly. Through the wood he asked whether anyone inside had an older relative for the waitlist.

A stranger's voice at another woman's door, asking the one question she had spent twenty-nine years not answering. Keep the hands still. Let him write the flat off as empty.

Natalie sat behind the locked door with her wrapped hands in her lap and said nothing. His steps went on up the stair. When the landing was quiet she picked the leaflet off the mat, folded it once, and put it in her pocket.

Then she went out into the frost and down through the old core, keeping off the rail. At the head of the canal steps she stopped. Below her the yard lay iced and still. There was no print on the steps, no track across the water, nothing leading toward the north lanes. Nobody was at the arch.

The cold she had brought in from the night went deeper on the iced stone. She had not slept. She sat down on the top step, and when she tried to stand she could not make herself. The sun came up over the core while she sat there. It was mid-morning before she walked back to the flat, having found nothing.
