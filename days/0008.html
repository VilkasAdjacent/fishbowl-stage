Day 8: The Girl Who Tied Her Arm Up With Her School Tie

Thursday 5 July 2046, dawn

Dear Parents, a number of pupils have reported tiredness and headaches this week. If your child is unwell, please keep them at home.a notice from the school nurse, taped to the gate glass at Linden Rise

The storm blew itself out before first light. The drizzle stopped, and a hard pale sky came up over the old core. The canal yard lay flooded and still. On the fringe estates the ground had gone white with frost.

Hyejin Park walked down to the old canal bridge in the cold. The storm had left a line of grit and leaf on the bridge footing where the water stood highest. She laid her hand flat between that line and the lip of the iron hatch. A hand's width to spare. The lid was down. She listened, and no sound came out of it.

Fucking held, then. Lid shut, yard dead quiet. The girl had had the sense to stay home. Nobody down here needed anything else from her.

She went back up the steps and home.

Some time after, the hatch lid came up. Isabel got herself over the iron on her elbows and knees and down into the yard. She had a cut half-done in the north lanes, and she set out to finish it. She told no one. She had no coat and no phone.

She got as far as the middle of the yard, the water to her shins. Her fingers would not close, and she could not keep her jaw still or her knees under her. She stood a while. Then she waded back toward the arch. The canal steps were empty. Nobody saw her start, and nobody saw her stop.

At Isabel's flat the door was locked from inside. Natalie sat at the kitchen table and peeled the wet towel off her palms. The skin had opened again under it. She wound fresh towel round each hand and taped it at the wrist. The spare coat hung by the door.

Towel off, wrap on. The fingers would not close, and that settled the steps, and the door, and the rest of it. Iz was out there. She was in here. This was the thing left in reach, and she did it properly.

At Linden Rise frost lay on the school gate, and the nurse's notice was taped to the glass. Amara Nwosu stopped there and the pressure in her head went up so fast her ears popped. It came from under the new science block, up out of the stormwater grate.

It was under the school. Under the science block, under four hundred kids, in daylight, and louder than anything she had ever had in her head. The notice was right there. Tiredness. Headaches. Keep them home.

Her ears kept ringing, and something beat slow and sick behind her eyes.

Before first bell she went round behind the bike sheds alone, got her fingers under the grate and heaved it up. Something in the dark of the culvert came at her and opened her forearm through the sleeve. She let the grate drop and ran. Behind the sheds she bound the arm with her school tie, tight, and pulled the wet sleeve down over the knot. When the bell went she went in with the rest.
