Day 2: The Girl Who Was Called Away Across the Water

Tuesday 3 July 2046, night

Old core residents: do not cross flooded canals on foot. Move to higher floors. Do not reply to this message.a municipal flood alert, sent 1:12 a.m.

Past midnight the river went over the lip of the old core's lowest canal. Water stood in the street to the height of a kerb, then a step.

Natalie and Isabel were at a corner in the north lanes. Something in the brickwork there might have needed cutting. Neither had decided. Then Natalie's patch stirred near the canal bridge, hard and low, and her line pulled her south across the city. She went. She told Isabel to wait and not to touch it.

Isabel waited eleven minutes. Then she put on the line's shape and made the cut herself. The thing in the brick had grown nerves. Nobody had reported it, so it had no number. Her hands shook before she started. Halfway through they gave out. The cut hung open in the wall, copper at its edges, not finished.

She stayed in the line's shape after that, standing in the water.

Natalie came back up the lanes at a run, wet to the knee. She stepped between Isabel and the wall and took the cut out of her hands. It bit back. A burn went across both of Natalie's palms, copper-dark, on top of the cold. The cut closed crooked.

The patch at the canal bridge had gone quiet by then. No one went to see what it had done.
