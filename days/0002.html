Day 2: The Girl Who Stopped Halfway Down the Road

Wednesday 4 July 2046, afternoon

IT'S YOUR HALL. DON'T LET THEM DECIDE WITHOUT YOU.Hall-vote leaflet, handed out at the Culvert Road junction

By afternoon the drizzle had stopped. Amara Nwosu left the bench at Linden Rise and set out with her meter for the Tangle, to take a reading off something other than the grate. At the Culvert Road junction a survey van with a council sign and Second Growth on its door stood across the pavement, and a clump of people were handing out leaflets about the hall vote. The needle jumped past its felt-tip marks. She could not get by without pushing through them, the box shrieking, so she turned back. Her hands shook all the way.

In Natalie's flat, Isabel asked her to come to the cuts and stay close, so she would know where Natalie was. Natalie said she was not going near the north cut, and would not say why.

The north cut was a blight Isabel had opened and left before its root was dead; left alone, a seam closes back over. Because it would not wait, Natalie asked Isabel to finish it now, with her watching. Isabel held up her hands. They would not hold, she said.

Natalie took her coat and went alone. Isabel watched her stop halfway to the north cut.

Nat stood with the itch at the back of her neck. Go on, and Iz sat in the flat alone with those hands. Girls left alone did not keep. The waiting was the part she knew.

She did not go on. The north cut's seam closed a little further over.
