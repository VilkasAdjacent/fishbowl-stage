Day 16: The Girl Who Was Told the Lake Was Not Hers

Friday 13 July 2046, morning

Say Yes for everyone you never got to thank.a leaflet for the October vote

On Friday morning, in the eastern wards, Hyejin Park got a message from the office of Catherine Lau, the name on the Tangle fence. It was the first word from her in eleven years, written as if none had passed. Catherine knew Hyejin had been at the block that night in 2035. She had never thanked her, and would like to, on a stage at the Yes rally before October.

Hyejin read it at the beds.

Fucking thank her. Everybody lied to themselves about the cost, and this was the biggest one yet: that 2035 was a thing anyone got thanked for.

She pocketed the phone and worked the frozen mulch, never looking up at the old woman's kitchen window, so there would be nothing to answer and no one asking again. The morning came to more beds turned.

At Linden Rise the drain lake had skinned over. On its path Elspeth Hale asked Amara Nwosu about the arm she held stiff.

Amara said she had slipped on the frost coming from the bus stop. "Leave me alone." That was the test: give nothing, and nothing could be pulled out.

Elspeth went, then turned. "The line on this lake is not yours, Amara Nwosu." Yesterday she had entered the lake against that line herself. She said it to keep the girl off the ice, and the entry in her own hands.

She walked on without opening the register.
