Day 10: The Woman Who Gave the Camera an Answer

Saturday 7 July 2046, morning

Dr Lau is still with us. Ask her anything.a Continuity screen at the Tangle fence

A day on, the frost went to wet on the footpaths, and meltwater ran in the estate drains.

Before first bell at Linden Rise, Amara Nwosu cut her forearm twice, small and clean, on the line the grate had opened. The old cut reopened under the tie.

Small ones. Clean. Nobody had seen and nobody had asked, so it was still hers. The second had felt smaller than the first, and that only meant the standard was closer.

At break she bolted herself into a toilet cubicle, washed the arm, wound the tie tight over it, and came out with her sleeve down and nothing on her face.

The council unveiled the anniversary plaque at the Tangle fence: brass, with Dr Catherine Lau's name cut into it, her avatar talking from a blue screen beside it. A campaign journalist had his camera on Hyejin Park before he asked what she remembered of the night the block went green. She answered before she could stop herself, on the record. He had her face, her plot and her answer now.

Afterward she walked down to the old canal bridge. The lid of the iron hatch was down. She passed it without lifting it. Underneath, the freezing girl stayed where she was, off the lane.

Elspeth Hale went up into the north lanes to see the blight before anyone had registered it. The cut stood half-done. Past it the growth had spread, green over the ash, where the line had gone quiet.
